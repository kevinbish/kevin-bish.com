    Keeps the 116,000+ UN/LOCODE location codes accurate and published on schedule, and is leading its move to an open-source, version-controlled platform.

    full_description();

    UN/LOCODE is the United Nations Code for Trade and Transport Locations: more than 116,000 five-character codes that identify ports, airports, inland terminals and trading places. Customs administrations, port community systems, maritime single windows, airlines and carriers all depend on it, and it is increasingly written into regulation, including maritime, emissions and sustainability reporting regimes. As Secretary, Kevin is responsible for keeping that reference data accurate, consistent and published on schedule.

    Day to day, the role sits between governments and industry. Kevin coordinates with Member States, National Focal Points, international organizations and private-sector users to receive, validate and publish Data Maintenance Requests under UNECE Recommendation No. 16, and supports the Advisory Group and Maintenance Team in setting priorities. Where regulators reference UN/LOCODE, he helps them apply it correctly so that the code list can function as trusted digital public infrastructure.

    He runs the release cycle end to end: setting the cut-off schedule ahead of each UN/CEFACT Forum, coordinating internal and expert review of location submissions, maintaining the DMR database and the online submission platform, and publishing each directory release (such as 2025-1) shortly after the Forum. He drafts the Advisory Group's parliamentary documents, organizes and documents its meetings, and follows up on its decisions, with a focus on the Programme of Work areas for policy, technology, data integrity and ICT re-engineering. He also supports the review of Recommendation No. 16 and produces procedures, guidance and training material for National Focal Points.

    The other half of the job is renewal. Kevin is leading the move from a legacy system to an open-source, version-controlled maintenance environment built with UN OICT and UNICC, aimed at better automation, auditability, transparency and cybersecurity compliance. That work includes geolocation tagging and geofencing of locations, and exploring AI and machine learning to help validate incoming requests. UN/LOCODE is among the first UNECE digital public goods being moved onto the UN Secretariat's GitLab-based open-source platform, alongside the UN Transparency Protocol (UNTP) and the eTIR National Application. Kevin coordinates the due diligence with OICT, UNICC and the UN Open Source Community of Practice: software bills of materials, software composition analysis, cybersecurity assessments and licence checks. The aim is recognition as a Digital Public Good, in line with the Global Digital Compact. In parallel he is working on long-term financing, including the contribution-based funding mechanism endorsed at the UN/CEFACT Plenary, and on capacity building through global workshops so that developing economies take part on equal terms.

He runs the Collaborative UN/CEFACT Environment (CUE) and the UNECE trade pages, and publishes standards through Bitbucket and Bamboo pipelines.
